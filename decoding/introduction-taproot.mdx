The idea of Taproot came from a post by Gregory Maxwell to the bitcoin-dev mailing list on 23 January 2018.

Nearly four years later, on 14 November 2021, it went live at [block 709,632](https://mempool.space/block/709632), through an activation method called Speedy Trial.

<ExpandableAlert title="How Speedy Trial worked" type="info" expandable={false}>
Speedy Trial gave miners a fixed signaling window.

-   Window: 24 April → 11 August 2021
-   Threshold: 1,815 of 2,016 blocks in a difficulty period (90%)
-   Locked in: June 2021
-   Activation height: 709,632, fixed in advance

So there was a five month gap between lock-in and activation.
That gap was for node operators to upgrade.
Bitcoin Core 0.21.1 and later enforce the rules.
</ExpandableAlert>

Three blocks later, in block 709,635, the first Taproot spend appeared. It was a **key path** spend, the very path you're about to spend the next few sections learning to build.

It has two outputs. One is an OP_RETURN: _"I like Schnorr sigs and I cannot lie. @bitbug42."_ The other creates a fresh P2TR. Click output 2 below to see it.

## Three BIPs

The idea of Taproot was turned into three BIPs:


-   **BIP340** Schnorr Signatures
-   **BIP341** Taproot
-   **BIP342** Tapscript

Keep these apart in your head. A lot of things people call "Taproot rules" are BIP340 rules that Taproot just inherits.

There's a lot going on here, so let's break it down.
