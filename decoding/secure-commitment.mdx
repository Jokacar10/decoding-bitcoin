Let's examine the cause of our insecure commitment first.

We have:

<div className="text-center my-4 text-orange-500">
  $$Q = P + t \cdot G$$
</div>

The root cause is that nothing ties $t$ to $P$.

Alice can pick any contract she wants, and then compute a $P'$ that makes it fit the same $Q$.

Let's fix that.

## The fix

One small change: put $P$ inside the hash.

<ScriptBlock
    code={`t = hash(P || contract)
Q = P + t·G`}
/>

Now let's take the same scenario, where Alice wants to swap the contract.

She needs a new key $P'$ and a new tweak:

<div className="text-center my-4 text-orange-500">
  $$t' = \text{hash}(P' \, || \, \text{contract}')$$
</div>

For her forgery to work she needs the same $Q$, so she needs:

<div className="text-center my-4 text-orange-500">
  $$P + t \cdot G = P' + t' \cdot G$$
</div>

She knows $P$, $t$, and the fake contract'. The only thing missing is $P'$. So let's solve for it:

<div className="text-center my-4 text-orange-500">
  $$P' = P + t \cdot G - t' \cdot G$$
</div>

<div className="text-center my-4 text-orange-500">
  $$P' = P + \text{hash}(P \, || \, \text{contract}) \cdot G - \text{hash}(P' \, || \, \text{contract}') \cdot G$$
</div>

Look at the last line.

$P'$ appears on **both sides** of the equation.

{/* TODO image */}

To compute $P'$ she first needs $t'$. But $t'$ is a hash of $P'$. So she needs $P'$ before she can get $P'$.

Alice can't pick $t'$ and solve for $P'$.

Her only other option is to guess: try random $P'$ values until one happens to work. That means breaking the hash function.

The commitment is now binding.

<ExpandableAlert title="This is what taproot really does" type="info">
This is exactly the shape taproot uses. The real tweak is

`t = tagged_hash("TapTweak", P || merkle_root)`

Same idea, the key is inside the hash. We'll build the real one later.
</ExpandableAlert>
